Release notes: {release}

This release extends the collection back to 2010. It classifies the magnitude of the product change in every collected AWS announcement from {first} to {cutoff}: {records} announcements in all. The release date is not the collection cutoff: the release holds nothing published after {cutoff}.

The classification describes the size of the announced product change itself. It does not measure customer consequences, engineering effort, or business value. AI readers produced the classifications and the audit references.

The 2010–2020 labels come from a different review protocol from the 2021–2026 labels, and that protocol rates higher. Read any comparison across 2020 against the bridge sample results.

What changed in this release

  • 8,421 announcements from 2010–2020, collected from the AWS directory API on 27 November 2026, each with a magnitude decision.
  • Two independent AI readers rated every 2010–2020 announcement. A third reading settled each of the 303 disagreements.
  • A blind audit of 300 hidden-sample records from 2010–2020, and a bridge sample of 400 records from 2021–2026 read again under the 2010–2020 protocol. The 2010–2020 audit page reports both.
  • 40 records at levels 4 and 5 drawn for a check by the maintainer. The maintainer's readings are not yet recorded.
  • The explorer now starts in 2010. The yearly charts mark three periods: pre-COVID (2010–2019), COVID (2020–2021) and post-COVID (2022–2026). With a service filter on, the page shows how many announcements in each period have no service tag.
  • A new public dataset with every record, the same nine fields, and no announcement text. The data guide defines the fields. The file from the previous release stays at its address.

The 9,247 labels for 2021–2024 and the 4,920 labels for 2025–2026 are unchanged.

The intervals describe sample uncertainty. They do not account for bias that the AI readers share. Agreement at level 4 is weak in every audit. In the 2021–2024 sample, the initial classifications missed both reference level-4 records, and the sample held no reference level-5 records.

Bridge sample: how differently the protocols rate

On the 400 bridge records, the 2010–2020 protocol agreed with the recorded labels on 323, or 80.8%. Its disagreements mostly moved records up: 35 went from level 2 to level 3, and 7 went from level 3 to level 2. Level 3 is 14.0% of the recorded labels and 20.8% of the new ones.

Counting 1 January to {cutoff_day} in each year, level 3 is 17.4% of pre-COVID announcements and 12.5% of post-COVID ones. That fall is no larger than the bridge drift, and it runs in the direction the drift predicts. It is not a finding.

Checks completed

The release checks confirm unique source addresses and a magnitude decision for every record. They confirm that every 2010–2020 decision replays from its saved readings, and that the 2021–2026 decisions are unchanged. They confirm that the public dataset holds every record with the same levels, reasons and bases as the full release, and that it holds no announcement text. A separate check confirms that this site's counts equal the release's verified annual, monthly, and per-service counts.

Limits that remain

  • All labels are AI judgments. The audits measure agreement with AI references, not accuracy validated by people.
  • Collection coverage has not been proven exhaustive. Archived index pages link 79 announcements from 2010–2020 that the AWS directory API no longer lists, 58 of them from 2012. 2017 coverage is unchecked.
  • The 2010–2020 protocol rates higher than the 2021–2026 passes. Comparisons across 2020 mix any real change with that difference.
  • Level 4 and level 5 trends are provisional. The maintainer's check of 40 such records is not yet recorded.
  • The early years are small: 66 announcements in 2010 and 82 in 2011.
  • Service tags are unknown on 2,106 records. Between 77% and 98% of each year's records from 2010–2014 have no tag. Service tags can overlap, so service totals must not be added together.
  • 232 records are marked needs review: 6 from 2010–2020, 204 from 2021–2024 and 22 from 2025–2026.
  • Announcement counts are not counts of unique features or products.
  • Search on this site covers titles and reasons only, because the dataset does not hold announcement text.

Treat category trends, and above all level 4 and level 5 trends, as provisional until rare categories and collection coverage have had broader independent review.
