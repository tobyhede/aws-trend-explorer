Scope of the product change

Classify the operation, interface, subsystem or offering that AWS added or modified. A customer's ability to remove infrastructure does not increase the rating of a parameter change.

LevelEvidence required
1 · Parameter changeA limit, default, performance characteristic or supported catalog value changes within an existing capability.
2 · Capability refinementControls, options or compatibility extend an existing operation. New controls can require new API fields without creating a separate capability.
3 · New capabilityA new operation or interface provides a distinct capability within an existing product.
4 · Major product expansionA new execution or operating model, or a substantial subsystem, broadens the product. Naming and number of API operations alone are insufficient.
5 · New productA distinct offering customers can adopt, supported by AWS product positioning. Pricing is supporting evidence.
Needs reviewThe announcement text does not establish the product change sufficiently.

Classification status

All 4,920 announcements from January 1, 2025 through October 28, 2026 received individual readings of saved AWS source text under this rubric. Three AI readers worked on separate batches without prior classifications. A separate reader assessed a seeded 120-record sample before seeing those ratings. Original readings and subsequent adjudications are retained.

The build replays explicit URL-keyed decisions and checks the exact source text against the saved review inputs. It does not infer ratings from earlier scope or facet labels. Reproduction means replaying these recorded judgments, not expecting a fresh model run to produce identical judgments. The historical extension adds individual source readings for all 9,247 records from 2021–2024. The 2010–2020 extension labels all 8,421 records from 2010–2020 under the same rubric, with two independent readers on every record and a third reading for each disagreement, bringing magnitude coverage to all 22,588 collected announcements. That protocol differs from the earlier passes, so a 400-record bridge sample from 2021–2026 was read again under it: it rates higher, with level 3 at 20.8% of the new labels against 14.0% recorded. The existing 4,920 recent decisions are preserved exactly. A fresh 200-record historical sample is independently read and adjudicated before its reference is compared with the initial classifications. Historical audit results and limitations.

Pure price changes, retirement, geographic rollout, compliance and educational announcements receive NA and remain in overall totals. Billing functionality is assessed normally. A new pricing choice within an existing reservation operation is a refinement; a price cut alone is NA. Stage is separate: GA-only changes are not automatically counted as new products, and substantive GA additions are assessed on their own evidence. Unclear GA deltas may remain U. Counts measure announcements, not unique launches.

Routine runtime, model or hardware catalog additions are level 1. New supported targets, options, protocols and bulk variants of existing operations are generally level 2. A first integration that introduces a distinct operation or interface is level 3; an additional target of an established integration is level 2. A new API name alone does not establish level 3. Mixed announcements receive the highest substantive level supported by their text.

The 2025–2026 comparison denominator includes levels 1–5 and U, with NA separate. The 2010–2026 monthly composition chart includes all records, with NA explicitly visible. Its share view uses all filtered announcements as the denominator. Levels are ordered categories, not numerical weights: level 4 does not mean four times the engineering effort or value of level 1. Classifications describe announced product changes, not AWS's internal implementation effort.

Reviewed examples

  • Lambda asynchronous payload limit from 256 KB to 1 MB is level 1, because the existing invocation ceiling changes.
  • Transfer Family custom SFTP ports is level 2, because existing servers gain port and handshake controls.
  • Lambda durable execution telemetry export is level 3, because the service gains an export capability.
  • Lambda Web Functions is level 4, because AWS introduces a compute primitive with HTTP-server programming, concurrent request handling and active-CPU billing.
  • Lambda Managed Instances and durable functions are level 4, reflecting changes to execution and programming models.

Older customer-consequence magnitude files and architecture-adjusted estimates have been retired from the active explorer. They are historical artifacts and do not measure this rubric.
