How we assigned scope

One English AWS announcement URL counts once. Scope coverage begins January 1, 2025 and ends October 28, 2026. Annual comparisons end October 28 in both years. November and December 2025 contribute to the monthly chart and rolling averages.

What the audit found

Two readers classified 48 enhancements independently from their AWS titles and bodies. The readers agreed on 46/48. Reader A subsequently reconciled the two readings against the source text. The proposed automated scope agreed with those decisions on 17/48. A second audit evaluated a before/after source-reading heuristic on 64 fresh announcements. The explorer applies reviewed decisions from both samples. A subsequent source-reading pass classified the 3,505 previously unresolved announcements. Its independent validation is linked above.

The deterministic sample selected eight rows per year from each of three groups in the matched periods: integration, performance or service limit, and other. Integration took priority for overlapping labels. Raw agreement describes this sample. It does not measure population accuracy or prevalence.

Existing product and regional-availability release types map to new product and extended respectively. Commercial and retirement announcements map to operational. Explicit maintenance wording can also map a software release to operational. The backlog pass examined version releases, unknowns and non-product posts individually. This sample tested enhancement scope only. Missing historical posts or errors in upstream labels can affect counts.

Each row retains its enhancement labels, the reason for its scope assignment, and a stage extracted only when the title explicitly mentions preview, general availability or lab mode. Stage is unspecified otherwise. These charts count posts, including separate posts for different stages of a capability.
