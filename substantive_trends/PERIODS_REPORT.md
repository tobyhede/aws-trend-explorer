# Substantive output by period

This is an AI reading of periods.txt, written by a Claude Code agent from the computed tables only. It is evidence, not a finding the maintainer has endorsed.

## Summary

The tables measure a proxy for shipped product change: how AI readers levelled AWS "What's New" announcements. They do not measure organisational performance or productivity, and say nothing about cause: not COVID, not layoffs, not anything else.

Volume grew in every period, and counts at levels 3–5 and 2–5 are higher recently than before 2020. In later periods the level 3 share is lower and the level 2 share higher, but the measured reader drift is at least as large. The level mix does not show a real change in what was shipped.

## Volume

Announcements rose from 48 in 2010 to 1,359 in 2019 and 2,257 in 2026. Growth slowed. The median year-on-year change was +45.1% pre-COVID and +40.8% in 2015–2019, against +11.8% post-COVID.

## Substantive output

Counts at levels 3–5 rose from 25 in 2010 to 215 in 2019. They peaked at 326 in 2020, fell to 181 in 2022 and 163 in 2024, then rose to 310 in 2025 and 423 in 2026. Counts at levels 2–5 went from 714 in 2019 to 943 in 2022 and 1,422 in 2026.

The median year-on-year change at levels 3–5 was +16.0% pre-COVID and +22.7% post-COVID. At levels 2–5 it was +33.3% and +19.6%.

The jump from 163 in 2024 to 310 in 2025 crosses a change of labelling pass (historical to recent). So does the fall from 326 in 2020 to 230 in 2021 (two-reader protocol to historical pass).

## Level mix

Level 1 is steady, from 12.2% to 12.7%. Level 2 rises from 37.3% pre-COVID to 42.7% and then 46.9%. Level 3 falls from 17.4% to 14.3% and then 12.5%. Level 4 stays near 1.1% and 1.0%. Level 5 falls from 2.6% to 1.3% and then 0.3%. U rises from 0.0% to 1.3% and 1.4%. NA falls from 29.3% to 26.9% and 25.3%.

## Reading the differences

Pre-COVID labels come from the two-reader protocol, which the bridge tested. Post-COVID labels come mostly from the recorded historical and recent passes. So a gap in shares mixes any real change with protocol difference.

The bridge shows that difference is large. On the same records, the level 3 share was 14.0% recorded against 20.8% under the two-reader protocol used for 2010–2020. Levels 3–5 were 15.0% against 24.2%. Moves from level 2 to 3 (35) far outnumber moves from 3 to 2 (7). Overall agreement was 80.8%.

The fall in level 3 share, from 17.4% to 12.5%, is not larger than the bridge gap of 14.0% against 20.8%, and runs in the direction the drift predicts. The rise in level 2 share matches those moves, so it too may be drift. The bridge prints no separate level 5 figure, so the fall in level 5 cannot be checked against it. The U share is near zero only under the two-reader protocol, which suggests a protocol effect.

Drift also differs within the post-COVID period. Agreement was 76.5% for 2021-2024 and 85.0% for 2025-2026. At levels 3–5, recorded against two-reader labels were 14.5% vs 25.5% and 15.5% vs 23.0%. That fits the 2024 to 2025 jump being partly a change of pass. Audit agreement was 90.2% for 2010-2020, 88.5% for 2021-2024 and 83.3% for 2025-2026, so readers also disagree within a protocol.

## Limits

- Labels are AI judgments. The agreement figures show AI readings agreeing with each other, not accuracy.
- Coverage is what the AWS directory API listed on the collection date. Archived index pages show links the API no longer lists, including 58 in 2012. 2017 is unchecked. 2021–2026 coverage rests on earlier collection checks.
- Reader drift is real, as the bridge shows. It holds only 2021–2026 records, so it cannot detect drift specific to older texts.
- Hindsight: readers know what products became. The protocol asked them to ignore this, but its influence cannot be ruled out.
- Early years are thin: 48 announcements in 2010 and 62 in 2011. Changes from such small bases swing widely.
- Level 4 and 5 counts are small. Trends at those levels are provisional.
