Fresh sample agreement

The initial ratings matched a blinded AI reference on 177/200 (88.5%) announcements; the 95% Wilson interval is 83.3–92.2%. These scores are measured before corrections. On reference levels 1–5, agreement is 123/145 (84.8%).

Two independent readers agreed on 184/200 ratings. A third source-only reader checked all disagreements and low-confidence cases plus 20 agreements. The adjudicated reference contains 5 unknown or low-confidence cases. The first reference was frozen before targeted review. During that review, an earlier official Wickr preview exposed one mistaken inaugural-launch assumption; its reference changed from 5 to U before sample scoring. The original frozen reference gives 176/200 agreement, versus 177/200 after that contextual correction. Both references are retained. This is AI-reference agreement, not human-validated accuracy; shared interpretation errors remain possible.

The 200 records were selected without replacement by a seeded pseudorandom URL ranking before classification. All four years were eligible. The saved seed, source files, original readings, reference and scoring code allow exact replay. Small level-4/5 samples cannot establish their accuracy.

If every unknown/low-confidence reference case were judged against versus in favor of the initial classification, agreement would range from 86.5% to 89.0%. This is a reference-uncertainty sensitivity range, not a confidence interval.

Rare-category limitation: the sample contains two reference level-4 announcements, both missed by the initial ratings, and no reference level-5 announcements. The targeted check of existing high ratings cannot establish recall among lower-rated records. Fine-grained major-expansion and new-product trend claims remain provisional.

Interpretation and coverage

Levels describe the product change itself, not implementation effort or consequences. Regional, pricing-only and non-product notices are outside scale (NA); U remains unresolved. A post is one observation even when it announces several changes. Annual comparisons use January 1–October 28 for all six years; calendar totals and October 2026 must not be treated as equally complete periods. Service tags overlap. Historical corpus completeness remains based on the existing collection validation and limited AWS roundup spot checks.
